# AI copy vs. professional copy: the comparison

Inputs:
- `ai-copy-audit.md`: 10 context-free AI sites, ~11.8k words
- `../research/pro-copy-tech.md`: 18 tech/product sites, Wayback 2014–2021
- `../research/pro-copy-brand.md`: 35 brand/hospitality/agency/portfolio/fitness sites, Wayback 2014–2021
- `../research/ai-tells-and-craft.md`: the Wikipedia AISIGNS catalog, 10+ existing anti-slop skills, and the classic craft canon

## The surprising part: the devices overlap

People usually assume AI copy is "elevate your journey" mush. In 2026 that's no longer true. Our ten sites use concrete nouns, real-looking prices, short sentences and plain CTAs, and that's what the pros do too. The pros also use the exact devices that look like AI tells:

| Device | AI sites | Pro sites |
|---|---|---|
| Fragment pairs | "Short menu. Long list of regulars." | "Develop. Preview. Ship." (Vercel), "Be narrow. Be good." (Hiut) |
| Negation run | "No spreadsheets. No guilt." | "No flashy logos. No senseless details." (Allbirds), "No GMOs. One more time. No GMOs." (Oatly) |
| Contrast frame | "Freshness isn't a slogan here." | "IT'S NOT FITNESS. IT'S LIFE." (Equinox), "the best jeans we can, not the most jeans we can" (Hiut) |
| Precise numbers | "12 kg per batch", "40 steps to the water" | "six-pound batch roaster" (Blue Bottle), "8 vaccines" (Stumptown), "16x per day" (Stripe) |
| Wry aside | "They hold spoons now." | "(We don't recommend this.)" (Warby Parker), "They've been through so much." (Stumptown) |

**So a ban list can't separate them.** Banning "No X. No Y." would delete Allbirds and Oatly. The difference is somewhere else.

## Where the difference actually is

### 1. Provenance: pro details are true, and you can tell they are
Pro specifics come with a story behind them: Kolshitsky in 1683 Vienna, beans mistaken for "camel feed", a Peugeot 505 wagon, "Thank you Mr Pinto", "Chelsea... who is actually a person". They're odd and checkable, and some are slightly embarrassing. AI specifics are plausible and tidy: a 12-kilo roaster named Gus, a sail loft from 1884, "38% fewer re-runs". They're invented to *look* specific. Every AI site fabricated its stats, testimonials, founders and client logos. **On a real business this is the most serious failure, because it's lying, not just bad style.**

### 2. Frequency: pros spend a device once, AI uses it in every section
Hiut's refusal line is *the* line on the page. In the AI set, the two-fragment heading appears 18 times across 9 sites, the number-pair heading 8 times, contrast frames 14 times, and a wry closer ends most paragraphs. A pro page has one or two signature moves and plain copy elsewhere. AI pages apply the same moves evenly everywhere.

### 3. Voice: the pros are all different, the AI sites all sound alike
Oatly is self-aware and absurd. Aesop is formal and literary ("labour-wearied hands"). Aman is proper nouns and restraint, and never says "luxury". Paul Graham uses zero adjectives. Ueno is a meme ("Much digital, very agency"). Tony's is lowercase activism about slavery. Stripe is dry and technical. HEY is a founder letter. **Ten pro sites, ten different writers.** The ten AI sites read like **one writer**: warm, modest, gently wry, a bit literary, gently anti-corporate. A CI tool and a yoga studio share a register. This is the strongest tell, and no word list can catch it.

### 4. Risk: pros pick fights, admit failure, and get weird
- **Enemies:** "the grotesquely swollen octopus of global capital" (Mother), "Most CSS frameworks do too much." (Tailwind), "an old man behind a wooden desk" (Oatly), "Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo, and Apple just let it happen." (HEY)
- **Failures:** innocent nearly went bankrupt and says so, Patagonia lists its own pollution, Blue Bottle's first model was "fraught", Linear says other platforms are "in the future".
- **Weirdness:** coined words (procrastiworking, cappoatccino, chocoshop, bananaphone, Fruit Towers, "Head of Mothering", "silent sports").
- The AI sites have none of this. Their "failures" are cosmetic ("six months rebuilding"), their enemy is "most apps", and their only coined word is the brand name, which they then pun on in every section.

### 5. Proof: pros borrow it from outside, AI makes it up
Pros attribute praise to verifiable outsiders: "Richard Vines, Bloomberg", "Jeremiah Shaw, Google", "Founder of Vulfpeck", Basecamp's "59%" paired with a named church. Luxury pros (Aman, Aesop, Soho House) show **no testimonials at all**. Every AI site has exactly three or four flawless testimonials, each with a setup, a number and a punchline, and the same names keep turning up across independent sites (Priya on 7/10, Daniel Okafor on 3, the company "Fieldnote" on 3).

### 6. Shape: pro pages are uneven and shaped by an idea
innocent's About page is a timeline of jokes. HEY's homepage is a letter. Mother runs the kitchen-table motif through everything. Intelligentsia's homepage is basically a catalogue. Aman has no prices. Pros leave sections out. The AI pages all follow one skeleton: eyebrow, claim heading, two-line lede, 3–4 cards, stat strip, testimonials ×3, pricing with a "Most popular" badge, FAQ, a pun in the footer.

### 7. Where the humour lives
Pro humour sits in microcopy and self-deprecation: the cookie banner ("Cookies go nicely with oat drinks."), the webcam ("updates sporadically whenever it feels like it"), the DOB field ("Sorry if this seems a bit nosy."), "I am still waiting for my first Kids' Choice Award." AI humour is a joke at the end of each paragraph, and its cookie banners, form errors and empty states are never written at all.

### 8. Headlines
Pro heroes come in at least 14 forms: greeting ("Hello, we're innocent"), measurable mission ("100% slave free"), product news ("Two new ways to blush."), question, imperative, customer as headline ("for teams who put robots on Mars"), enemy statement, narrow superlative, place and year, pun with a spec underneath. AI heroes use about two: the two-fragment claim ("Know what you can spend. Then go spend it.") and the scene-noun line ("Sleep where the sails were made.").

### 9. Vocabulary
Both sets avoid the classic AI words. The new AI tells are *virtue* words that perform sincerity: **honest (8×), actually (10×), real (8×), calm (6×), quiet (5×)**. The Wikipedia AISIGNS page (Oct 2026) independently flags "quietly, steady, practical, clearer, dependable, matters". Pros show sincerity with an admission ("We are not a perfect company, not even close") and never use the word "honest" about themselves.

## What this means for the skill

1. **The core problem is missing input, not style.** The fix has to start before drafting: collect real facts, real customer words, real proof, a real enemy and a real flaw. Anything missing becomes a visible `[NEED: …]` placeholder and is never invented.
2. **Choose the voice on purpose and treat the default as banned.** The skill has to name the AI house voice (warm, wry, modest, Monocle) and make the writer pick a register based on the brand, from a menu of real registers.
3. **Set a budget for devices instead of banning them.** Each signature move (fragment pair, negation run, contrast, wry aside, pun) can be used once per page, where it lands hardest.
4. **Let reader questions and the brand's organising idea set the page shape.** Don't use the template, and leave sections out when nothing true can fill them.
5. **Put the voice into the microcopy.** Write the cookie banner, form errors, empty states, the 404 page and the confirmation email.
6. **Catch the second-order tells:** stat strips, the default cast of names, footer puns, polished testimonials, virtue words, number-pair headings, the converted-building origin story, response-time promises.
7. **Prevent over-correction.** Every ban gets a positive move and a check for the habit that replaces it, and Orwell's rule 6 applies to the rules themselves.
8. **Use outside eyes to evaluate.** A lint script for countable tells, a swap test against competitors, a cross-page sameness check, and a fresh-context reader instead of self-scoring.
