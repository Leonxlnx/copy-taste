# Voices: a menu of real registers

Choose one primary register per brand. You can borrow a single move from a second one. Each register comes from pre-LLM sites (2014–2021 Wayback snapshots), and the quotes are kept short.

**Default to none of them.** Choose by the brand's audience, price point and real character. When the brief doesn't settle it, pick the register that's *least* like the last page you wrote.

---

### 0. The default AI register (the anti-example)
- **How it sounds:** warm, wry, modest, a bit literary, gently anti-corporate. Most paragraphs end on a deadpan twist. Sincerity comes through "honest / calm / quiet / actually". You get a stat strip, three polished testimonials and a pun in the footer.
- **When it fits:** almost never by default. If the user asks for it, use it, and still respect the device budget.

---

### 1. Facts only, no adjectives
- **Exemplars:** Paul Graham's bio ("Paul Graham is a programmer, writer, and investor."), Pinboard, Soho House ("Applicants must be nominated by two existing Soho House members.")
- **Moves:**
  - Plain declarative sentences with names, dates and counts.
  - Third person, or a terse first person.
  - Exclusivity or quality is shown by a rule or a fact.
- **Fits:** strong track records, professional services, B2B infrastructure, private clubs, investors, consultants with real credentials.
- **Fails when:** the facts are weak. Then it reads as a CV with nothing in it.

### 2. Technical-dry with proof
- **Exemplars:** Stripe ("we deploy our production API 16x per day"), Linear ("Built for speed" over "< 100ms"), Tailwind ("Most CSS frameworks do too much."), Panic's Transmit
- **Moves:**
  - A label paired with a hard figure.
  - The mechanism told in verb-led steps.
  - Real class names, commands and UI strings on the page.
  - An enemy that is a category.
  - Jargon kept on purpose for experts, or translated once.
  - The product is the grammatical subject.
- **Fits:** developer tools, infrastructure, APIs, technical SaaS.
- **Fails when:** it turns into a spec dump with no pain named, or uses abstractions like "power and alignment".

### 3. Founder letter, opinionated
- **Exemplars:** HEY ("It's 2020, we need to talk about email." / "And Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo, and Apple just let it happen."), Basecamp ("We've been expecting you." / "Hair on fire, buried under email, stuff everywhere.")
- **Moves:**
  - First person singular or plural.
  - A named incumbent as the enemy.
  - The reader's pain in their own words.
  - Refrains ("You're in control.").
  - Long-form argument is fine.
  - A human sign-off.
- **Fits:** products with a stance, indie software, anything founder-led and against the status quo.
- **Fails when:** the stance is fake, or the founder has no real argument with the incumbent.

### 4. Self-aware absurd
- **Exemplars:** Oatly ("this incredible computerized oatmilk locator" / "Hope you are okay with that."), innocent ("Call the bananaphone" / "How to go bankrupt, quickly"), Ueno ("Much digital, very agency.")
- **Moves:**
  - Admits it's marketing.
  - Uses hype only ironically.
  - One real person named as a running gag ("Chelsea… who is actually a person").
  - Coined house words.
  - Funny headline over a factual body.
  - Microcopy in full voice.
  - Failures on display.
- **Fits:** consumer packaged goods, drinks, snacks, small agencies, playful consumer apps.
- **Fails when:** the jokes are random, aimed at the customer, or put on a serious purchase such as finance or health.

### 5. Literary-formal
- **Exemplars:** Aesop ("labour-wearied hands and cuticles" / "In appreciation of a father's fiction" / "Sincere thanks for subscribing.")
- **Moves:**
  - An elevated but exact vocabulary.
  - "Clients", not "customers".
  - Sensory notes as tight lists ("Citrus, woody, herbaceous").
  - Botanical or material names.
  - Never an exclamation mark.
  - The price sits plainly inside the button.
- **Fits:** premium skincare, fragrance, design objects, high-end retail.
- **Fails when:** it gets ornate without being exact, or drifts into "luxurious experience" mush.

### 6. Proper-noun restraint
- **Exemplars:** Aman ("Formerly the king's guest house" / "overlooks the 9th-century Buddhist monument of Borobudur"). The word "luxury" never appears in Aman's body copy.
- **Moves:**
  - Places, history, architecture and geography do the work.
  - Unhurried physical verbs: reside, overlook, stroll.
  - No prices in the hero and no testimonials.
  - The brand story runs three sentences and gives a date.
- **Fits:** luxury hospitality, architecture practices, heritage properties, galleries.
- **Fails when:** there's no real history or place to name.

### 7. Sensory, second person, present tense
- **Exemplars:** Stumptown ("your hand is warmed by the mug, you bring it up to your nose" / "Stare. Smell. Five seconds later, repeat"), Barry's ("when the doors close, lights dim, and music turns up"), Y7 ("There are no mirrors in the candle-lit studios.")
- **Moves:**
  - Puts the reader's body in the scene.
  - Shows craft as repeated process and constraint.
  - One defining absence ("no mirrors").
  - Odd human numbers (45 seconds, 8 vaccines).
- **Fits:** coffee, food, bars, fitness, yoga, spas, anything you consume physically.
- **Fails when:** every section turns sensory. Pricing and booking should still be plain.

### 8. Historical scene and homage
- **Exemplars:** Blue Bottle (Kolshitsky in 1683 Vienna, beans "thought to be camel feed", "your Peugeot 505 wagon"), Dishoom ("Students had breakfast. Families dined. Lawyers read briefs.")
- **Moves:**
  - Opens on a vanished world or a dated moment, not on the brand.
  - Furniture, streets and people named.
  - Captions as evidence ("dates from 1914").
- **Fits:** restaurants, heritage brands, anything with a real lineage.
- **Fails when:** the history is invented or generic ("rooted in tradition").

### 9. Activist, plain-spoken
- **Exemplars:** Patagonia ("Build the best product, cause no unnecessary harm" / CTA "Shop Used Gear"), Tony's Chocolonely ("together we'll make chocolate 100% slave free" / "It won't be easy. And it's pretty complex.")
- **Moves:**
  - A one-sentence mission you can measure.
  - The hard subject said plainly, with no euphemism.
  - Admits its own harm.
  - CTAs that act on the mission.
  - Proof in numbers and annual reports.
- **Fits:** mission-led consumer brands, non-profits, B-corps with real numbers.
- **Fails when:** the mission is a paragraph of abstract nouns ("inspire healthier communities").

### 10. Refusal and narrowness
- **Exemplars:** Hiut Denim ("Do One Thing Well." / "Saying no is focus." / "can only make 100 pairs of Hiut's a week")
- **Moves:**
  - Positions itself by what it won't do.
  - Scarcity stated as an honest limit.
  - Makers get titles ("Grand Masters.").
  - Rituals as community ("No Wash Club").
- **Fits:** makers, small-batch goods, specialists, single-product companies.
- **Fails when:** the refusal is invented to sound focused.

### 11. Product as hero, showcase
- **Exemplars:** Apple, iPhone 11 ("It just got a whole lot harder to take a bad photo." / "water resistant up to 2 meters for up to 30 minutes"), AirPods Pro ("Arrival of the fittest." over "three sizes of soft, flexible silicone tips")
- **Moves:**
  - Almost no "we".
  - The product is the subject of every sentence.
  - A pun is always followed directly by a spec.
  - A moment from life ("your dog catching a frisbee").
- **Fits:** hardware, physical products, apps with one hero feature.
- **Fails when:** a pun has no fact underneath it.

### 12. Hurried and conversational
- **Exemplars:** Ueno ("We know you are busy so we'll get straight to the point." / "Or don't. It's your life." / CTA "Tell us everything"), Warby Parker ("squinting and complaining. (We don't recommend this.)"), Frank Chimero ("I am still waiting for my first Kids' Choice Award.")
- **Moves:**
  - Talks to one busy person.
  - Asides in brackets.
  - Self-deprecation.
  - Casual section labels ("Awards and stuff").
  - Gives the reader permission to leave.
- **Fits:** small studios, freelancers, portfolios, DTC brands with a founder story.
- **Fails when:** it becomes chummy ("We get it.") or the jokes replace information.

## Matching voice to category: starting points only

| Category | Try first | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Café / roastery | 7 Sensory, 8 Historical, 4 Absurd | 0 Default |
| Dev tool / infrastructure | 2 Technical-dry, 3 Founder letter | 4 Absurd in the hero |
| SaaS for small business | 3 Founder letter, 11 Showcase | Abstract "power and alignment" |
| Agency / studio | 1 Facts only, 12 Hurried, 4 Absurd | Category-noun stacks ("digital brand experience innovation company") |
| Portfolio | 12 Hurried, 1 Facts only | The credential list with no person in it |
| Luxury hotel | 6 Proper-noun restraint | Any use of the word "luxury" |
| Independent hotel | 12 Hurried (Hoxton's "Shoebox, Cosy and Roomy"), 7 Sensory | "boutique", "curated experiences" |
| Fitness / yoga | 7 Sensory, 10 Refusal | "best version of yourself" |
| Architecture | 6 Proper-noun restraint, 1 Facts only | "spaces that inspire" |
| Course / education | 3 Founder letter (the teacher's voice), 7 Sensory for physical skills | Outcome hype ("transform your life") |
| Skincare / beauty | 5 Literary-formal, 10 Refusal, Glossier-style customer-as-brand | "clean", "glow-up" without ingredients |
| Finance | 1 Facts only, 3 Founder letter | Jokes about money stress, unsourced savings figures |
| CPG / drinks | 4 Absurd, 9 Activist | The default register |
