# Pro moves, section by section

These come from 53 sites archived on the Wayback Machine between 2014 and 2021. Quotes are short and kept for analysis. Treat them as moves to learn from, not lines to copy.

---

## Hero: 14 forms the pros actually use

Most pro heroes are 2–9 words long and have one clause. None stacks two adjectives. Pick the form on purpose. If the last page you wrote used "Claim. Twist.", use a different form this time.

| # | Form | Examples | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Greeting / self-introduction | "Hello, we're innocent" · "Hi! We're Glossier." · "We are COLLINS." | The brand voice is the product |
| 2 | Talking to the reader's state | "We've been expecting you." (Basecamp) | You know why they arrived |
| 3 | Measurable mission | "together we'll make chocolate 100% slave free" (Tony's) | You have a mission you can measure |
| 4 | Product news, plain | "Two new ways to blush." (Glossier) · "The all-new Things" | There's real news |
| 5 | Question to the reader | "Where Will Your Cold Brew Bundle Take You?" · "Got a project?" (Ueno) | The reader is browsing and low-intent |
| 6 | Imperative or mantra | "Send Better Email" (Mailchimp) · "Do One Thing Well." (Hiut) | One clear action or belief |
| 7 | Category + unexpected promise | "The issue tracking tool you'll enjoy using" (Linear) · "Social Bookmarking for Introverts" (Pinboard) | The category is known and the twist is real |
| 8 | Customer as the headline | "A messaging app for teams who put robots on Mars" (Slack) | You have one famous or striking customer |
| 9 | Infrastructure noun + scope | "Payments infrastructure for the internet" (Stripe) | Serious B2B with real scale |
| 10 | Enemy statement | "Most CSS frameworks do too much." (Tailwind) | The reader already resents the incumbent |
| 11 | Outcome without the pain | "Rapidly build modern websites without ever leaving your HTML." (Tailwind) | The pain is specific and well known. This uses a contrast, so count it in the budget |
| 12 | Place + year anchor | "The original Hoxton, a firm fixture in Shoreditch since 2006." | Venues and anything rooted in a place |
| 13 | Twisted idiom with a spec under it | "We didn't reinvent the wheel, only email." (HEY) · "Arrival of the fittest." (AirPods, over the ear-tip sizes) | Only with a fact directly underneath |
| 14 | Narrow, checkable superlative | "the world's largest independently-owned design studio" (Pentagram) | It's true and you prove it in the next line |

**Subhead:** one sentence, usually 12–25 words. It either names the category with three real verbs or gives one proof point ("billions of dollars", "8 million people"). It never stacks adjectives.

## Section headings

- **Label + claim pair.** A scannable label with a one-line claim under it: "Battery" over "Charge wirelessly. Use tirelessly."; "Component-driven" over "Worried about duplication? Don't be." This is the pro default, not the two-fragment heading.
- **Imperatives** are the most common heading type on SMB and productivity pages: "Find your people", "Send videos quickly", "Set direction with Roadmap".
- **Full-sentence claims** carry the argument: "Never lose a file again", "We literally wrote the book on working remotely."
- **Statistics as headings**, only when they're real, and each one paired with a named quote: Basecamp's "59% have fewer weekly meetings."
- **Questions** are rare. Keep them for the FAQ, the closing CTA, or a kicker set up for a two-word answer ("Not enough light? No problem.").
- **About-page headings** can be odd. Numbers and questions work ("319 years later", "May we bring you coffee?", "Who are our people?"), and so do jokes ("The fairly boring bit", "How to go bankrupt, quickly").

## About / story

1. **Start in a specific moment, not with a thesis.** Examples: 1683 Vienna (Blue Bottle), a festival stall with two bins (innocent), glasses lost on a backpacking trip (Warby Parker), lunch around a kitchen table (Mother).
2. **Structure it as anecdotes, a timeline or questions, not value pillars.** The weakest pro about page in the sample (Sweetgreen) is the one built on "Our Core Values" pillars.
3. **Put failure and luck in the plot.** Every bank turned them down. The first business model was "fraught". A lucky investor gets thanked by name.
4. **Write founders as characters, not titles.** "a slightly disaffected freelance musician and coffee lunatic" · "an itinerant climber selling pitons from the back of his car".
5. **Express values as behaviours with numbers.** "up to four times" the commodity price, "at least 1% of our sales", "nominated by two existing members".
6. **End on a modest present tense.** "Improbably and delightfully, there are hundreds of us now." · "That's it really."
7. **Name the team and say how to reach them.** "Reach out to Daniel, Adam, Serah, or Mandi today."

**Portfolio about page:**
- A one-line bio with three roles, one with a deflating adjective ("multi-disciplinary designer, accidental writer, and lapsed illustrator").
- What you make and for whom.
- One or two signature projects with dates.
- One credo sentence.
- A list of odd interests that mixes high and low (Vermeer, pizza, dogs).
- Availability as a plain date.
- Or skip all of that and give pure facts, Paul Graham style.

## Features / what we do

- **Mechanism in verb-led steps.** "Push your code" → "We build, test and deploy it" → "Every deployment gets a URL."
- **Outcome first, then the feature as proof.** "Never Drop The Ball Again", then "just choose a time — for example, Monday at 8:55 am".
- **A moment, not a use case.** "Send your entire wedding video to family with a simple link."
- **Real values in examples** (times, sizes, amounts), not "customizable".
- **Show the product doing the work:** real UI strings, commands, class names, a real menu.
- **Product taxonomy is copy too.** "Shoebox, Cosy and Roomy" (Hoxton rooms), "Decaf Librarian's Blend", "Fruit Towers".
- **Block length:** a 2–8 word heading, then 1–3 sentences (about 15–45 words). Deeper explanation sits behind the scannable layer. Vary lengths across blocks.

## Proof

- **Let other people say the superlatives**, with full attribution: "Richard Vines, Bloomberg", "Jeremiah Shaw, Google", "Founder of Vulfpeck".
- **Testimonials that sound like a person:** "i always regret it when i don't use gumroad." (lowercase, as written). Rough beats polished.
- **Pair a statistic with a named human quote** so each one vouches for the other.
- **Show a rule instead of a claim.** Soho House's two-nomination rule proves exclusivity with no adjective.
- **Luxury and craft often show no testimonials at all**, and the work carries the proof.
- **Never** use logo walls of unnamed clients, "as seen in" with no outlet, or a 4.9 rating with no source.

## Pricing, FAQ, security: the plain zone

- **Tone drops to plain facts.** Readers here are anxious.
- **Priced, literal perks.** "Late checkout for £5 an hour" (Hoxton), "Sign up for $11 / year" (Pinboard).
- **Put risk reversal next to the button, and only if it's true.** "No obligations, no credit card required."
- **Admit limits right in the FAQ.** "Support for other platforms will be added in the future." · "There's no upgrade discount, as our last paid update was in 2010."
- **Ask the buyer's real objection out loud and answer it flatly.** "Is ZEIT Now going to lock me in?"

---

## CTAs

- **A verb plus the real object.** "Try 5 for free", "Book a room tonight", "Find your club", "Tell us everything", "Start selling with Gumroad".
- **Literal even when playful.** "Computerized oatmilk locator", "to our chocoshop!"
- **A graceful exit for browsers.** "I'm just looking, I'm not buying" (Hoxton).
- **The closing CTA restates the hero's promise with a real number**, or is a plain question ("Ready to get started?" is fine once, at the bottom).

## Microcopy: where pros put their best lines

- Cookie banner: "Cookies go nicely with oat drinks." / "Ok with me, let's go!" (Oatly)
- Date-of-birth field: "Sorry if this seems a bit nosy." (innocent)
- Webcam: "(The camera updates sporadically whenever it feels like it)" (Hiut)
- Newsletter: "Get secret notes" (Stumptown); "Once a month or so I send out a letter" (Craig Mod)
- Confirmation: "Sincere thanks for subscribing." (Aesop, in register)
- Nav descriptions: Work = "It comes first." (Wieden+Kennedy)

Write these whenever the page has them. A generic cookie banner under a hand-tuned hero gives the game away.

---

## Footer

Facts go here: address, phone, company or registration number, links. If you add a human line, keep it plain ("Enjoy the rest of your day!") or make it a real fact ("© mmxv pg"). Don't end on a pun built from the brand's metaphor.

---

## Numbers and humour

- **Odd human numbers beat round big ones:** "eleven people" on the first newsletter, "8 vaccines", "19 partners", "45 seconds".
- **Every number is real and sits in a sentence**, next to the claim it proves.
- **Humour is aimed at yourself, never the customer.** It lives in brackets, asides and microcopy, and it's never a quip at the end of every paragraph.
- **Use rhythm on purpose, once.** Examples: a run of fragments ("Students had breakfast. Families dined. Lawyers read briefs. Writers found their characters."), a list that ends on a surprise word ("better, weirder, and more human"), or one absurdly long sentence next to a deadpan short one.
