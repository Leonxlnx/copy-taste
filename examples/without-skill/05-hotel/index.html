The building

Built in 1884. Restored one beam at a time.

For sixty years, the Ashby Sail Loft cut and stitched canvas for the schooners that worked this coast. The top floor was left open so a full mainsail could be laid flat, and the round window at the gable end let the sailmakers check the wind before they started.

When Nora and Tomas Ruel bought the building in 2016, it had been a boat-storage shed for decades. They spent three years on it. The original spruce beams stayed. So did the sailmakers' pencil marks on the north wall, which you can still read from the stairs.

What they added was quiet: good beds, thick walls between rooms, real hot water, and windows big enough to make the harbor part of the room. There are no televisions. Nobody has asked for one yet.

The harbor

Four good ways to spend a morning.

Port Ellery is small enough to walk end to end in twenty minutes. Here is what we tell guests to do first. Ask at the front desk and we'll set any of it up for you.

01

Row the peapod

Take our cedar rowboat around the moorings before the wind comes up. Life jackets and a thermos of coffee are in the boat.

Free for guests · 6–9 a.m.
02

Walk to Ellery Light

A flat shore path from our front door to the 1857 lighthouse at the point, past tide pools and a bench that's always empty.

2.1 miles each way
03

Sail the Margaret Ames

A two-hour afternoon sail on a 1912 wooden schooner, crewed by our neighbors at Ellery Schooner Co. Guests get first pick of seats.

$68 per person · June–Sept
04

Sit in the dock sauna

A wood-fired sauna at the end of our pier. Heat up, climb down the ladder into the Atlantic, and repeat until it feels reasonable.
