What changes

By March, the sky stops being wallpaper.

Most people quit astronomy in the first month because they buy too much gear and can't find anything. This course goes in the other order: skills first, gear later, if ever.

01

Find things on purpose

You'll star-hop from bright stars you already know to clusters, nebulae and a galaxy 2.5 million light-years away, without an app pointing for you.

02

Know what you're seeing

Why one star looks orange and its neighbor looks blue. What the Moon's ragged edge tells you about its mountains. Which "star" is really Jupiter, and the four moons it brought along.

03

Keep going on your own

You'll leave with an observing log, a list of 60 targets for every season and a simple routine for planning a night in under five minutes.

The syllabus

Eight weeks, one sky at a time.

Every week has a short lesson to watch indoors and one specific thing to go and find outdoors. The order follows the winter sky, so what you learn is overhead when you learn it.

WEEK 1

Your eyes in the dark

Dark adaptation, averted vision and why a red flashlight matters. You'll be surprised how much more you see after twenty patient minutes.

Orion's Belt and Sword
WEEK 2

Holding binoculars properly

What 7×50 and 10×50 actually mean, how to brace for a steady view, and a two-minute test to see if your pair is in good shape.

Capella and the Kids
WEEK 3

The Moon, slowly

Reading the terminator, naming the seas, and catching earthshine. The Moon is the best teacher you have, so we give it a full week.

Craters Copernicus and Tycho
WEEK 4

Star-hopping

The one skill that separates people who look up from people who find things. You'll learn to read a chart, measure the sky with your fist and hop in short, sure steps.

The Orion Nebula, M42
WEEK 5

Clusters and star colors

Why stars come in families, and what their colors say about their temperature and age. Binoculars show clusters better than most telescopes do.

The Pleiades and the Hyades
WEEK 6

Planets and their moons

Telling planets from stars at a glance, and following Jupiter's moons as they change places from one night to the next.

Jupiter's four Galilean moons
WEEK 7

Faint fuzzies

Galaxies and nebulae are dim, grey and astonishing once you know what you're looking at. We cover light pollution, sky quality and timing your night.

The Andromeda Galaxy, M31
WEEK 8

Your own observing plan

Keeping a log, planning a session in five minutes, and a target list for spring, summer and autumn so the course doesn't end when the winter sky does.

Your instructor

Imogen Varga has spent 16 years handing strangers a pair of binoculars.

For most of that time she ran the public nights at Harlow Ridge Observatory, where her job was to take people who'd never looked up on purpose and send them home pointing at Saturn. Somewhere around her ten-thousandth visitor she noticed that the people who kept going weren't the ones who bought telescopes. They were the ones who learned to find their way.

"I don't want you to memorize the sky. I want you to be able to walk outside on any clear night and say hello to it."

Imogen holds a master's in science education and writes the monthly "What's Up This Month" column for the Northern Plains Astronomical Society. She observes from a suburban backyard with a pair of 10×50s she bought secondhand in 2009.

Questions

Good questions we get a lot.

Still unsure? Write to hello@binocularsky.com and Imogen will answer you herself, usually within a day.

I live in a city. Is this still worth it?

Yes. About half of past students observe from cities or suburbs. Binoculars cut through light pollution better than you'd expect, and every week includes targets chosen to work under a bright sky. Week seven covers how to get the most from a short trip somewhere darker, if you ever want to.

What if my binoculars aren't good enough?

Almost any binoculars will show you the Moon's craters, Jupiter's moons and the Pleiades. In week two you'll run a simple test on your pair. If they really are holding you back, we'll suggest specific models in three price ranges, but we earn nothing from any of them.

I'm in the Southern Hemisphere. Does it work for me?

Mostly. The core skills are the same everywhere, and Orion, the Pleiades and the planets are visible from both hemispheres. Each week includes a southern alternative target, such as the Jewel Box cluster or the Magellanic Clouds, for students below the equator.

What happens on cloudy weeks?

They happen to everyone, so the course is built for them. Each lesson's outdoor target stays visible for several weeks, and the cohort's live sessions always include an "if you missed it" segment. You can catch up whenever the sky clears.

Can I share it with my kids?

Please do. One enrollment covers your household. The lessons are made for adults but work well for curious kids aged ten and up, and many families do the outdoor sessions together.

What if I can't make the live Sunday sessions?

Every session is recorded and posted within a few hours. You can send questions in beforehand, and Imogen will answer them on the recording.
