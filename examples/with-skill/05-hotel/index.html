Track side or garden side

The building has two faces, and the price follows the noise. Pick the side that suits how you sleep.

Track side

Rooms 1 to 4€88 a night for twoDogs allowed

Windows on the square, the station and platforms 1 to 3. From the bed you can read the station clock. Freight trains pass through the night; the windows are double-glazed, and there are earplugs in the bedside drawer.

Room 1 is one flight up, for anyone who'd rather skip stairs. Room 4 looks straight down the platforms and is the one regulars ask for.

Garden side

Rooms 6 to 10€112 a night for two€124 with a bath

Windows on the courtyard, where Rosalba grows the summer tomatoes for breakfast, and on the hill of the Sacro Monte Calvario behind it. You'll hear the chapel bell at seven and a neighbour's radio on Sunday mornings.

Rooms 9 and 10 have a bath. Room 6 is the smallest in the building, so it's €98.

Every room has a shower, a desk lamp bright enough to read a map by, Wi-Fi that reaches the top floor, and this month's printed timetable on the back of the door. There's no room 5. The story is further down.

Tell us which train you're catching

Most people who stay here arrive on one train and leave on another, so the desk keeps the station's hours and watches its boards.

  1. When you book, give us the train you plan to leave on. If you don't know it yet, tell us where you're going.
  2. The evening before you leave, whoever is on the desk checks it against the Trenitalia and SBB boards and slides a printed slip under your door: train, platform, any change, and the next train if yours runs late.
  3. Check out by 11:00. Your bags wait in the old room 5 until your train goes, even if that's the last one of the day.
  4. If your train is cancelled and nothing else leaves that day, your next night here costs half.

The night the last train to Brig didn't run

Rosalba Ferraris ran Bar Sempione, on the corner of the station square, for twenty-two years before there was a hotel above it. One Thursday in February 1996 the last train to Brig was cancelled a few minutes before it was due. The waiting room was locked at midnight, and the eleven people left on platform 1 crossed the square to the only lit window, which was hers.

She took them upstairs to her flat. Four slept in the two beds, three on the sofa and the floor, three at the kitchen table with their heads on their arms, and one, a cheese buyer from Bern called Herr Zbinden, in the bath, with a pillow (he asked for the bath). In the morning she made eleven coffees on the bar machine and walked them across to the 6:12. Herr Zbinden left 50,000 lire under his saucer with a note: Für das Zimmer. For the room.

She opened four rooms the next spring. That first year, room 5 sat directly above the freight sidings, and nobody who was given it got any sleep. It holds luggage now. The bar closed in 2011 after losing money for longer than the family likes to admit; breakfast is served where the counter used to be.

Rosalba is 84. Her son Ettore runs the desk and writes most of the slips. She still does breakfast on Saturdays, which is market day in Domodossola, and the torta di pane is hers.

In Italian station announcements, a coincidenza is a connecting train. Rosalba liked the other meaning as well.

Breakfast starts at ten to six

The first trains to Switzerland leave before seven, so the coffee goes on at 5:50. Until 6:30 it's a tray you can take back to your room. After that the full table is laid out in the old bar until 10:30, and most of it comes from the Ossola valleys within forty minutes of here.

Leaving before 5:50? Ask the night before and we'll leave a paper bag at the desk with bread, cheese, an apple and a flask of coffee.

Before you book

Is there a lift?

No. The rooms are on the first and second floors, and it's 46 steps to the top. Whoever is on the desk will carry your bags. If stairs are hard for you, ask for room 1, which is one flight up.

How loud is the track side?

Loud enough to matter if you're a light sleeper. Freight runs through the night and the first passenger train leaves just after five. The windows are double-glazed and the earplugs are free. If you'd prefer not to hear trains at all, book the garden side.

I'm arriving after the desk closes.

We'll text you a code for the side door on the afternoon you arrive. Your key will be in an envelope with your name on it, on the bar.

Is there parking?

We have none of our own. The station car park is on the square, paid by the hour or by the day, and it's a two-minute walk.

Can I bring my dog?

Yes, in the track-side rooms, for €10 a night. Bring their bed; we have bowls.

Do you serve dinner?

Breakfast only. For dinner we send guests to Trattoria Bruna, two streets behind the square. It's closed on Mondays, so on Mondays we send them to the pizzeria next door to it.

What if my train is cancelled?

If nothing else leaves that day, your next night is half price. If you're stuck in Domodossola and weren't staying with us, ring the desk anyway. We usually have a room, and we've had a bath before.
